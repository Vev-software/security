01 — Executive summary

A map worth protecting

Atlas gives you one living, queryable view of your entire IT landscape: systems, dependencies, ownership, lifecycle and risk. That single view is exactly what a regulator, an architect — and an attacker — would each find valuable. This paper describes, in plain terms, how we keep that view useful to you and useless to anyone else.

Three choices sit underneath everything that follows:

  • You choose where your data lives.
    Run Atlas fully self-hosted, hosted by us inside the EU, or air-gapped. The map is only ever assembled where you decide it should be.
  • Security you can audit, not just trust.
    Atlas Community is open source (AGPL-3.0) and the data and import/export formats are published as open contracts (Apache-2.0). Your team can read the code that receives, stores and separates your data before installing it.
  • Deny on doubt.
    Access is decided by an offline evaluator that fails static: on error or uncertainty it closes down rather than opening up.
This is a living document. It is updated as roadmap controls land; the version and date on the cover tell you which revision you are reading.

02 — Security principles

The rules we design by

  • Data sovereignty first.
    European by values: you retain control of residency and can keep the platform entirely within your own boundary. Self-hosted installations make no call home.
  • Least data.
    A map should describe your architecture, not store your secrets. We deliberately model as little sensitive detail as possible.
  • Evidence over promises.
    Open source, open contracts, signed provenance and an SBOM mean claims in this paper can be checked, not merely believed.
  • Fail static.
    Security-relevant decisions default to closed. A failure denies access; it never silently grants it.
  • Portability is a control.
    Open formats and full export mean you are never locked in — you can always take your map with you.

04 — Architecture & data flow

How data moves through Atlas

Atlas ingests or captures a description of your estate, holds it in a tenant-scoped catalogue, and serves views over it. Every request is bound to your tenant, every security decision runs through an offline evaluator, and every change and export is written to an append-only audit log.

This diagram is deliberately high-level. Detailed component and data-flow diagrams are available in the assurance pack (Section 13).

05 — Tenant isolation

Separated at the source Live

All data is separated per organisation at the source: every request is bound to your tenant, and the isolation sits in several layers so that a single slip in the code cannot leak data across tenants. In hosted deployments each customer relationship is isolated; in self-hosted deployments the entire instance is yours.

06 — Access control, fail-static & audit

Deny on doubt, record everything

  • Fail-static evaluator. Live
    Access is decided by an offline evaluator that denies on doubt and never grants on error. Self-hosted and air-gapped installations evaluate entitlements from a signed snapshot, with no live dependency on our control plane.
  • Append-only audit log. Live
    Changes and exports are recorded in an append-only log you can read yourself. A full export of your landscape can never happen silently.
  • Role-based access. On the way
    Fine-grained roles — for example, architects with write access and customer teams with read-only views of the same landscape — are being rolled out; this section will move to the present tense once confirmed live.

07 — Encryption

In transit today; at rest on the way

  • In transit. Live
    Data is encrypted in transit.
  • At rest (hosted). On the way
    In our hosted environment, data will also be encrypted at rest, with the most sensitive fields protected separately.
  • At rest (self-hosted). Live
    For self-hosted installations we document how you encrypt your own storage, so encryption at rest is entirely under your control.

08 — Data minimization

A map, not a vault Live

Atlas is designed to describe your architecture — systems, dependencies, ownership, lifecycle — not to hold your secrets. We keep as little sensitive detail as possible, which shrinks both the blast radius of any incident and the surface a regulator has to assess.

09 — AI governance

AI with control, not AI at any price On the way

Atlas' AI features are optional and pass through a governed gateway. The guarantees below describe how they are being built; we will state them in the present tense only once they are confirmed live.

  • No training on your data.
    When enabled, landscape data will not be used to train third-party models.
  • EU-resident inference.
    When enabled, inference is kept EU-resident.
  • Sensitive content filtered.
    When enabled, sensitive content is filtered before it reaches a model.

10 — Secure development & supply chain

Built to be checked

  • Open source, auditable. Live
    Atlas Community is AGPL-3.0. Your security team can read the code that receives, stores and separates your data before you install it.
  • Open contracts. Live
    The data model and import/export formats are published as open contracts under Apache-2.0 — which is what makes portability real rather than a promise.
  • SBOM & signed provenance. Live
    We attach a software bill of materials and signed provenance to our releases, so you can verify what you are running and where it came from.
  • CI security gates. Live
    Builds run automated build, test and format gates. Static analysis and additional scanning are being expanded.

11 — Vulnerability disclosure

Report privately, fixed coordinately Live

We operate a coordinated disclosure process. Report vulnerabilities privately — not in a public issue. We coordinate the fix and disclosure case by case, and we credit reporters who want it. The current policy and process live in our public security repository.

Report a vulnerability: follow the policy at github.com/Vev-software/security (see SECURITY.md). Do not disclose in a public issue.
